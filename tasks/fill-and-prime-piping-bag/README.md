# Fill a piping bag and scrape the spatula clean

A cuffed, tip-fitted decorating bag stands open in an ordinary tumbler. Load it with the portion of buttercream on the offset spatula. One gripper steadies and pinches the folded cuff while the other draws the blade through it. The technique transfers sticky icing into the bag and leaves the tool and outside of the bag clean for decoration. Either arm may take either role. [Wilton decorating bag method](https://wilton.com/how-to-prepare-a-decorating-bag/wltech-7846/) · [King Arthur Baking tumbler support](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/01/26/how-to-fill-piping-bag)

## Equipment and reset

Use a table 1110 by 1250 mm, with its surface 747.5 mm above the floor. X points forward from the YAM bases into the scene, positive Y points toward the left-arm side, and Z measures height from the floor. Set the bases 610 mm apart at `(0, ±305, 750)` mm.

- **Decorating bag and support.** Use a standard clean 305 mm disposable plastic bag fitted with a plastic coupler and smooth round stainless tip. Fold its top back to leave 175 mm between the tip and the folded edge. Set its point down in an open plastic tumbler centred at `(288, 22)` mm, 142 mm high with a 100 mm base and a 114 mm rim. The cuff remains about 43 mm above the tumbler. Open it to about 110 by 60 mm and turn its long axis 83° from +X toward +Y. Keep the upper cuff above the tumbler rim so the film can flex where the fingers and blade touch it.
- **Tool and rest.** Use an offset stainless-steel spreading spatula with a 92 by 27 mm working blade, 117 by 22 mm rounded polymer handle and about 22 mm total offset from its grip axis to the blade plane. A shallow ceramic rest about 117 by 72 mm supports the handle near `(213, -228)` mm, with the blade pointing 110° clockwise from +X in plan view. The blade and buttercream stay clear of the rest.
- **Portion.** Weigh 30 ± 1 g of ready-to-use decorating buttercream at 20–22 °C. Spread it centrally on the upper face of the blade over the final 55 mm of working length, keeping the handle clean. Wilton's ready-to-use decorating icing is a reproducible option.
- **Clean surfaces.** Use clean nitrile finger cots on the YAM pads if the rig is shared. Use a fresh bag and freshly portioned icing for each physical trial, or clean and dry the parts before resetting. Put the dry bag and coupler on the scale before loading so the filled weight can be tared without the tumbler. Start the arms in their ready poses with open grippers. A scale with 1 g resolution and one fixed photo of the cuff suffice for grading.

## Physical scoring

Use the final net mass within the bag and the final released arrangement. Compute the fraction as net mass divided by the original portion mass, capped to the range 0–1. A bag standing in the tumbler with zero icing earns zero.

- Score up to 0.8 as 0.8 times the fraction of the starting portion transferred to the bag.
- Award 1.0 for at least 87.5% transferred, the bag upright in its tumbler, the spatula at rest on its stand, and both grippers clear for 1 continuous second.
- Icing on the table and outside the bag is excluded from net mass. Deduct a tare for external icing if needed, by moving it off the outside with a clean scraper before weighing. Leave the bag and clean exterior available for a fixed photo.

The simulator measures native cuff and spatula deflections. Both jaws have to compress the flexible film, and the steel blade has to move out under load with the original loaded section having passed into the bag. At each physically cleared part of the blade, the displayed icing moves from the blade to the interior. This is an attachment-and-release model for sticky buttercream. The bag body occupies its real folded envelope and the cuff uses two spring-supported film sections.

## Run

Use the Isaac Lab-Arena environment pinned by `pyproject.toml`. Install the task with `python -m pip install -e . --no-deps`. The native reference and controls use seed 1415158438 with a 20 Hz policy and 240 Hz physics. The run limit is 720 actions.

```bash
python run.py --device cuda:0 --physics-device cpu --output /tmp/piping-native
python run.py --no-cameras --policy hold --output /tmp/piping-hold
python run.py --no-cameras --policy open-cuff --output /tmp/piping-open-cuff
```

The Inspect Robots embodiment is `piping_bag`. The reference in `run.py` uses measured blade and cuff positions to guide ordinary YAM commands. It supports the open cuff during insertion, then raises the spatula shoulder clear of the cuff before pinching the flat blade and withdrawing it. Each run records a score receipt.
