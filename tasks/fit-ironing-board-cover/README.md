# Fit an elastic-edged cover on a tabletop ironing board

Stretch and tuck a replacement padded cover over a tabletop ironing board before ironing. Each end must remain captured by the board once the arms stop holding it. The robot starts with both hems loose. The [HOMZ cover installation instructions][homz] show seating elastic under the nose before stretching it to the opposite end. The tabletop size matches the [73 × 32 cm JÄLL form factor][board]. The replacement cover is a matching generic elastic-edge cotton cover with a 6 mm foam pad.

## Setup and reset

Use an unheated board on a level bench. No iron is needed. Deck height is 105 mm above the worktop. The bench plane is at Z = 0.7475 m in the standard bimanual YAM frame, with X from the arm mounts toward the board and positive Y to the left. The deck center is at X = 0.372 m, Y = 0. The long axis runs along Y, with the narrow nose pointing to positive Y. The steel deck is 18 mm thick including the rolled rim. Its legs are 24 mm steel tubes with rubber feet, centered 102 mm on each side of the board's centerline at Y = −224 and +212 mm.

Lay the loose cover face up, aligned to the board's centerline. Place the two gathered end seams over the face at Y = ±308 mm, above the surface and clear of both rims. The cover is loose on the deck and no end is installed. The simple reset uses the board, matching cover, and the standard YAM worktop only. Mount both YAM arms with bases 610 mm apart, starting clear with grippers open. This fixed reset uses seed `1415158438`.

## Score a trial

Give 1.0 if the released cover sits flat and both ends remain secured for one second. Inspect underneath or use a side video with a small mm ruler. Both exposed elastic seam midpoints must lie below the steel rim and 13–79 mm inboard of the farthest point on the corresponding end. The center pad should sit parallel to the deck and within 12 mm of its installed height. Give 0.5 if one elastic end remains under the rim with both hands clear, and 0 otherwise. The simulator checks the same end geometry, level pad and one-second released settling window. Any arm roles or order are allowed.

## Run

Use the [official Isaac Lab-Arena and Inspect Robots setup][guide] with the YAM adapter installed. From this task's folder:

```sh
uv pip install -e .
python run.py --device cuda:0 --output runs/demo
python run.py --device cuda:0 --no-cameras --policy hold --output runs/hold
python run.py --device cuda:0 --no-cameras --policy lifted --output runs/lifted
```

At 20 action steps per second, the 520-action budget is 26 seconds of task time. The local runner reports actual elapsed wall time, final score and native measurements in `receipt.json` and records each step to `trace.jsonl`. It starts each camera file with frame zero and writes every subsequent RGB frame at 20 fps. The lower-cost headless controls are a stationary policy and a policy that leaves the heel hem lifted while releasing the nose.

## Simulation fidelity

Robot motions are ordinary 14-component YAM arm and gripper actions. The prismatic spring proxies represent stretch against both ends and compliance at the tucked hem. The board, sewn hems and center pad use native PhysX contact. The two moving padded textile panels are visual proxies for the cloth surface. Local fabric wrinkling and distributed forces are approximated by the native motion of the sewn ends. No extra grasp hardware is attached to the cover.
